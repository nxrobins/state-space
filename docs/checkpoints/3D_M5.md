## Goal and delivery gates

Make 3D terrain structurally interactive: bonded structures carry weight, lose
strength when heated, fracture when overloaded, and fall as coherent pieces,
with exact CPU/dense-GPU/active-GPU state and replay.

Completion requires all of the following, with retained source-bound evidence:

1. Six-direction reciprocal bonds, integrity, anchors, support and load transfer
   across storage seams. Contact never implicitly welds distinct fragments.
2. Cutting, overload and heating cause failure. Unsupported components translate
   downward, respect collision dependencies and boundaries, and transport all
   state coordinates by a permutation.
3. A stable bridge-over-lava control, cut and heated-support failures, and a
   load-bearing frozen crossing whose melting support fails. Fixtures specify
   expected outcomes and failure-tick bounds.
4. Complete CPU/dense/sparse equality, zero accounted energy drift, 1,024-tick
   structural scenarios and exact odd-tick restarts, including seam failures.
5. Distant dependency invalidation, partition/camera/order independence, and no
   publication of a tick with an unfinished structural solve.
6. Sequential 1920x1080 gameplay measurements: 60 warmup and 600 measured frames
   including intact, fracture and falling-debris phases. 32^3 and 64^3 require
   >=59.9 ticks/s, zero missed 60 Hz deadlines and zero lost display slots.
   Report the same full-frame metrics at 128^3 without a required 60 Hz gate.
7. A separate versioned API/snapshot contract, atomic validation, preserved older
   engine contracts, bounded verification and the continuing legacy quarantine.

## Rules under implementation

The new rules identity is `ca-3d-m5`, schema 2, world envelope
`finite-structural-1`. Existing `ca-3d-m1` entries keep their original law.
No older snapshot silently acquires bonds or new behavior.

Canonical coordinates use x + width * (y + height * z), with gravity toward +Y.
Three u32 coordinates hold packed material/thermal/phase, conserved energyQ and
structure. Structure uses integrity bits 0..7, reciprocal -X/+X/-Y/+Y/-Z/+Z
bonds in bits 8..13, and anchor bit 14. Bit 15 is a transient fresh-placement or
solidification marker, never legal in a completed snapshot. Remaining bits,
packed velocity and packed flags are zero. Supported materials match M1.

Only solid/frozen phases with catalog cohesion participate. Placement and new
solidification seed integrity 255 and bond to adjacent intact cohesive cells.
Later contact does not form bonds. Explicit bond edits are allowed. Melting
clears mechanical state; breaking preserves material and energy. A broken anchor
retains its pin until explicitly unpinned, replaced or transformed.

The support graph contains reciprocal bonds in six directions and upward
support through unbonded vertical intact-solid contact. Roots are anchors, the
closed floor, and bottom contacts too dense to displace. Breadth-first distances
are global. Each supported cell contributes ceil(density/64), at least one.
Loads propagate in descending support distance and split among all predecessors;
ascending canonical index receives any remainder. Root reactions exactly sum
to supported weight. Unreachable components have zero supported load.

Nominal strength is cohesion * 16. First compute floor(nominal * integrity / 255).
Then multiply by the remaining thermal fraction and floor: the fraction is one
below the material softening temperature and falls to zero over 64 temperature
units, using full energy-derived temperature. These two explicit integer rounds
are part of M5, keeping all products within u32 bounds at 128^3. Zero capacity
breaks the cell; overload removes min(integrity, max(1,
floor((load-capacity)*255/nominal))). Damage is simultaneous and edges are then
normalized reciprocally.

Post-damage bonded components fall one cell toward +Y unless blocked. Dependencies
between distinct vertically contacting components propagate blocking to completion;
unblocked components can move together without welding. Each vertical run rotates
its displaced bottom occupant into the vacated top, conserving every coordinate.
Intact/pinned cells cannot move independently in local material passes. Broken,
unpinned solids behave as grains. The 27 M1 movement/heat/phase passes follow,
with mechanical coordinates transported by every swap and new solidification
bonds normalized at the end. Heating/phase changes affect the next support solve.

Mechanical energy, rotation, elasticity, combustion, pressure/stored velocity,
streaming and reciprocal player forces are outside M5. The exact energy ledger
continues to account for thermal/latent/chemical energy, not gravitational work.

### GPU convergence and activity contract

Three complete state banks isolate the committed world from scratch work.
Support distances and component labels use monotone global fixed-point passes;
component blocking propagates to quiescence. Control readbacks verify convergence,
balanced root reactions and absence of solver errors before publication.
Iterative solves check a five-second deadline between batches, and each GPU
completion has a 1.5-second timeout. A failed or incomplete solve never advances
the committed tick.

A complete global plan can be reused only after an exact per-cell comparison
proves unchanged structural words, intact-cell densities and contact-root predicates,
and the resulting thermal state proves there will be no integrity loss on the next tick. Fluid density changes
away from structural contacts do not invalidate this certificate. This is an equality
certificate, not a hash. Any changed dependency invalidates the entire plan.
Edits and restores invalidate it unconditionally. Stable ticks use one completion
boundary. A complete batched fresh solve uses two; a rejected attempt falls back
to the general convergent solver and may use more. The default attempt budgets
were 16 support, 16 load, 16 component and four blocking iterations in the
baseline implementation (the follow-up below raises these budgets). GPU flags
must certify convergence and exact balanced reactions before publication.

Parallel monotone load relaxation converges on the support-distance DAG to the
same rounded shares as the independent CPU reverse-distance reduction. Component
relaxation hooks only representatives already in the same bonded component;
canonical indices strictly decrease until reaching its unique minimum. An
explicit one-iteration probe forces the fallback on eight-chunk X/Z beams and
compares complete CPU/GPU states, including the odd-tick restart. No truncated
iteration count becomes a physical approximation.

Active mode skips only locally certified material passes. Global structural
motion runs first; then both working banks agree and all cells are checked for
possible movement, heat exchange or phase change across every parity/direction.
Changes wake adjacent required chunks before the following pass. The canonical
GPU work list is immutable during a pass. Structural dependencies are solved
globally, independently of camera visibility. Global graph work is not yet
incremental; it is reused only under the exact certificate above.

Source inspection identifies concrete weaknesses in the verification harness:

- The watchdog performs synchronous disk flushes, output forwarding and OS
  queries in its deadline loop. A blocked call can prevent it from reaching
  the cancellation check. No saved stack identifies such a call as the cause.
- Replay polling creates repeated CLI processes and prints unchanged status.
  Those bytes reset the stall timer even without a new verified tick. Overall
  deadlines remain, but the progress deadline is weaker than its name suggests.
- Every tick reads back and repacks all three state arrays. At 64³ that is
  3 MiB per tick and at least 3 GiB over 1,024 ticks, plus CPU copies and
  allocation. Full-state comparison is necessary; this allocation pattern is not.
- Digests and timings are retained in browser memory until completion, while
  durable progress appears every 16 ticks. We cannot locate the failed tick or
  distinguish encode, submission, mapping, unpacking and hashing at interruption.
- The JavaScript GPU timeout depends on its event loop and is armed after
  submission calls. Job CPU limits govern CPU scheduling; they do not impose
  a GPU execution limit. OS/driver recovery is a separate mechanism
  ([CPU job limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_cpu_rate_control_information),
  [Windows GPU recovery](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/display/timeout-detection-and-recovery)).
- The stage's source-bound manifest is saved only after its child returns.
  A crash loses that run's own start manifest as well as its detailed results.

The recommended approach is to move hardware reproduction and full GPU
verification to a **separate physical GPU test host** while this workstation's
incident is unresolved. A software adapter can exercise the harness, but cannot
prove hardware parity or GPU performance. Results from different hardware must
be labeled accordingly; they cannot establish this RTX 4090 machine's latency.

Before any execution, review and implement these harness changes with the hold
still active:

1. Use one persistent browser connection or bounded HTTP progress channel.
   Record monotonic tick/phase sequences independently from observer heartbeats;
   repeated status must not reset progress deadlines.
2. Give the deadline watchdog its own process and keep synchronous disk writes,
   telemetry queries and logging out of its cancellation loop. Bound writer and
   telemetry queues; missing telemetry or a stalled writer must fail closed.
   This improves application containment, not recovery from a whole-host hang.
3. Persist a source/configuration manifest before launch and small phase events
   before/after submission, mapping and hashing. Save every completed digest
   incrementally. Label any unflushed tail unknown rather than inferring success.
4. Reuse typed buffers and canonical bytes while comparing every cell, energy
   value and structural word at every tick. Preserve the existing digest format
   and separately prove inspection independence. Do not substitute sparse
   sampling or an approximate checksum for complete-state verification.

Candidate diagnostic limits for review are a 15-second startup deadline,
10-second execution budget, 250 ms per verified tick, five seconds without
phase progress, three-second telemetry freshness, five-second teardown, and
an explicit 20 ms yield after every correctness tick. These are proposed
exposure limits, **not validated safety guarantees**. Retain existing resource
caps. Any failure stops the sequence and blocks retries; never auto-escalate
from a tiny pass to a larger job.

A short cold-start window around the last known progress is useful diagnostic
evidence, but it may miss a history-dependent failure. Pauses within a resident
world preserve its state; recreating the world tests snapshot continuation.
Keep those claims separate. Neither segmented replay nor throttled diagnostic
frames replace the uninterrupted 1,024-tick proof or the original 60-warmup /
600-frame gameplay measurement. Do not disable GPU timeout recovery, raise
limits, change drivers/BIOS, or run stress tests as part of this proposal.

## Safer harness implementation awaiting review (2026-10-16)

**Implemented in source; runtime validation remains blocked.** This change does
not establish that GPU execution is safe on this workstation. No builds, unit
tests, browser sessions, replay checks or benchmarks were run for this change.
The current `dist/verification/safety-hold.json` retains `blockRecovery: true`,
and no recovery permit was issued. Legacy quarantine is unchanged.

The new path has three independent responsibilities:

- `scripts/verification_browser.mjs` owns one fresh Chromium process, a temporary
  profile, one CDP WebSocket and a single outstanding Python request. It replaces
  repeated browser CLI launches. It never attaches to an existing browser. CDP
  messages, error storage and request/response queues are bounded; a broken
  connection fails the stage. The browser and helper inherit the workload Job.
- `scripts/verification_watchdog.py` is a separate process outside that workload
  Job and its CPU cap. It opens only the randomly named Job created by the
  supervisor. Its 50 ms loop checks a pagefile-backed control mapping; it performs
  no logging, file writes or telemetry queries. Three seconds without a supervisor
  heartbeat or the absolute stage deadline terminates the owned Job. The workload
  remains suspended until the watchdog has armed. The supervisor in turn refuses
  a missing or stale watchdog heartbeat. Disk or telemetry stalls in the supervisor
  therefore do not also block the independent cancellation check.
- `scripts/verification_journal.py` persists a sequence-numbered, SHA-256-linked
  `events.jsonl`. A bounded writer flushes and calls `fsync` before acknowledging
  an event or publishing progress. The browser has one HTTP request in flight and
  at most 64 queued events. An invalid sequence, full queue, writer error or missing
  acknowledgement fails the stage. The loopback HTTP server has at most eight
  handlers and a 4 KiB event limit.

The authored `level-trajectory` replay writes `step`, `snapshot`, and `hash` phase
boundaries and a complete-state digest plus step duration for **every compared
tick**, including restart ranges. The next tick waits for its predecessor's durable
acknowledgement, then yields for 20 ms. These phase names identify the outer SDK
operations; they do not claim to separate GPU-internal encode, submit, map and
unpack operations. The existing digest, CPU comparison, state coordinates,
commands and physics schedule are unchanged. Buffer reuse from the proposal is
deferred until these harness changes are reviewed.

The smaller foundation, shader and UI probes retain their existing correctness
scope and publish changed status events. They do not acquire a new per-tick proof
merely by using this transport. Repeated status and heartbeat text cannot reset
the structured progress timer. The authored replay and full frame benchmark
publish their own ordered events instead of using DOM status as progress.

The full benchmark journals all 60 warmup and 600 measured ticks through the
bounded background queue. It adds no per-frame wait for persistence. Logging
overhead and any resulting scheduling delay are part of the observed run; they
must not be subtracted. Final success requires the queue to drain and the saved
frame events to match the retained samples exactly. A missing tail, cancelled
frame, write error or receipt mismatch cannot pass. Replay receipts must likewise
match every durable tick digest and completed range. A readable final line after
a crash is not proof that its HTTP acknowledgement reached the browser; only a
complete, reconciled receipt establishes completion.

`recover_m5_verification.py` now durably saves `start.json` with source hashes,
command, configuration and limits, and an incomplete `report.json`, **before**
issuing a permit or launching a child. Hashes include the harness sources and
browser event client. The final receipt binds the start manifest. The browser
records its version, owned PID, headless mode and viewport. An interruption leaves
an explicitly incomplete run instead of relying on a manifest written at the end.
